Musical Design & Progression Bank

Summary of the chord vocabulary, keyboard voicing rules, and progression bank used by the trainer.

1. Root-in-Bass Triads (Major & Composite Minor)

Every chord is a 3-note triad with its root in the left-hand bass:

  • Major keys: I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii°
  • Minor keys: i, ii°, III, iv, V, VI, vii° — uses the standard classical composite minor: harmonic minor (raised ♯7) for the dominant family (V and vii°) so cadential tension toward i is clear, and natural minor (unraised ♭7) for the mediant (III major rather than III+).
  • Why plain triads with roots in the bass: V7 is omitted so students cannot rely on spotting a 4-note chord or tritone instead of listening for function, and the bass always plays the chord root so Stage 1 and Stage 2 map directly to the bass degree (no bass inversions or cadential I6/4 yet).
2. Keyboard Voicing & Voice Leading

Chords are voiced in 4-part close keyboard style (1 bass note in the left hand + 3-note close triad within one hand's span in the right hand):

  • Register & starting position: Left-hand bass stays in the baritone octave (E2–E3); right-hand upper voices stay in the middle register (G3–C5). Chord 1 starts in a randomized right-hand position (5/3, 6/3, or 6/4 upper shape, so the soprano is not fixed to one scale degree) and subsequent chords take the closest inversion.
  • Common tones & stepwise motion: Shared notes are held in the exact same voice while moving voices step by semitone or whole tone.
  • Contrary motion against stepping bass: When the bass steps by 2nd (e.g., IV → V, iv → V, or V → vi), the right-hand voices move in contrary motion to avoid parallel 5ths and octaves.
  • Cadential & minor rules: On authentic D → T resolutions, the leading tone (7 / ♯7) resolves up by semitone to 1. In minor keys, upper voices never leap across the melodic augmented second (♭6 ↔ ♯7) between iv/VI and V.
Current scope: All exercises currently play as simultaneous block chords with voice-led right-hand inversions and roots in the bass (so vii° and ii° also keep their root in the bass rather than vii°6 / ii°6). Pure root-position-only voicings, bass inversions, and arpeggiated figuration are not included yet.
