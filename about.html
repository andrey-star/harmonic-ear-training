Teacher’s Reference: Exercise & Theory Rules

A quick 1-minute summary of the musical rules, chord vocabulary, and voicing engine.

1. What chords are in the vocabulary?
All 7 diatonic triads in the chosen major key:
  • I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii° (triads only; no 7ths, inversions in bass, or chromaticism).
2. How are the functional families categorized?
Standard Riemannian three-function model:
  • Tonic (T): I, vi (submediant substitute), iii (mediant substitute).
  • Subdominant (S): IV, ii (supertonic pre-dominant). Both contain scale degree 4.
  • Dominant (D): V, vii° (leading-tone diminished). Both contain the leading tone (7).
3. How are chords voiced? (Voice Leading)
Pianistic close keyboard style, not chunky parallel root jumps and not wide choral spreads:
  • Left Hand: Grounded bass note in baritone range (F2 to G3, centered on C3).
  • Right Hand: 3-voice close triad centered around Middle C (span ≤ 9–12 semitones, fits in one hand).
  • Voice Leading: Common tones are strictly held in the same voice; moving voices step to the nearest chord tone (minimal distance algorithm).
4. How does the exercise test the ear?
Two-stage progressive breakdown:
  • Tonic Anchor: Plays a clean root-position I chord to lock the pitch center, followed by a pause.
  • Progression: Plays a 3-chord sequence.
  • Stage 1 (Core): Identify the functional family for each chord (Tonic, Subdominant, or Dominant).
  • Stage 2 (Optional Toggle): Identify the exact Roman numeral (I through vii°).
5. Are the 3 chords picked randomly?
The user can toggle between two modes in the header:
  • Syntactic (Default): Picks from classical progression archetypes (e.g., I–IV–V, ii–V–I, I–vi–IV, IV–V–vi, vi–ii–V, I–iii–IV).
  • Random Diatonic: Any 3 diatonic chords picked at random (without direct immediate repeats) for advanced exploration.
6. How does the synthesis avoid mechanical artifacts?
  • Voicing Balance: Bass has firm velocity (0.66), inner voices are softened (0.52–0.58) to blend, and soprano is not piercing (0.55).
  • Acoustic Decay: Piano-like ADSR decay with 25ms legato overlap to simulate a damper pedal connection.
