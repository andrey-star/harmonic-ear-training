Notes on Functional Harmony & Voice Leading

In functional tonal harmony (Hugo Riemann's system), every diatonic chord belongs to one of three primary functional archetypes:

Tonic Family (T)

Major: I, iii, vi · Minor: i, III, VI

Rest, home, resolution. The submediant and mediant share two pitch classes with the tonic triad and act as tonic substitutes or prolongations.

Subdominant Family (S)

Major: ii, IV · Minor: ii°, iv

Departure and pre-dominant forward motion. Both contain scale degree 4, naturally pulling toward the dominant.

Dominant Family (D)

Major & Minor: V, vii°

Tension and tonal drive. Both contain the leading tone (scale degree 7, raised in minor) and scale degree 2, urgently demanding resolution to the tonic.

Close Keyboard Voicing & Part-Writing

Common tones are retained in the same voice while moving voices step by minimal intervals, moving in contrary motion against stepping bass lines (E2 to E3) and avoiding melodic augmented seconds (♭6 ↔ ♯7) in minor keys.
